1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Statutory interest for late payment is simple interest at a rate equal to
   the reference rate plus at least eight percentage points (Source 1, Article 2(6)).
   For a euro country, the reference rate is either the ECB's interest rate on its most
   recent main refinancing operations or the marginal rate from the ECB's variable-rate
   tenders for those operations. For a non-euro country, it is the equivalent rate set
   by its national central bank (Source 1, Article 2(7)(a) and (b)). The reference rate
   is fixed twice a year: the rate in force on 1 January applies to the first half of
   the year, and the rate in force on 1 July applies to the second half (Source 1,
   Article 3(2)). Between businesses, interest runs from the day after the payment date
   or period fixed in the contract (Source 1, Article 3(3)(a)).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No, the sources do not support it. The directive gives a fixed sum of
   EUR 40, not 25, and calls it a **minimum** ("as a minimum, a fixed sum of EUR 40"),
   not a maximum (Source 1, Article 6(1)). It is compensation for the creditor's own
   recovery costs and is due once interest for late payment becomes payable under
   Article 3 or 4 (Source 1, Article 6(1) and 6(2)). On top of that fixed sum, the
   creditor may claim reasonable compensation for recovery costs above EUR 40, such as
   a lawyer or a debt collection agency (Source 1, Article 6(3)). The sources set no
   EU maximum. The 25 EUR figure appears only in the owner's note, which nobody has
   checked (Source 2, point 1). The sources do not cover how each country put this
   into national law; the extract is the directive as originally adopted (Source 1,
   header). Following the task rules, the reminder messages contain no fee.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. Between businesses, the creditor is entitled to interest for late
   payment "without the necessity of a reminder", provided it has fulfilled its own
   contractual and legal obligations and has not received the amount due on time,
   unless the debtor is not responsible for the delay (Source 1, Article 3(1)(a) and
   (b)). The same applies when the debtor is a public authority (Source 1,
   Article 4(1)). The fixed EUR 40 sum is also "payable without the necessity of a
   reminder" (Source 1, Article 6(2)). So the owner's belief that interest needs a
   reminder first is not supported (Source 2, point 2).
